Equipment Tray

These are the components used to capture a depth image and convert it into a 3D point cloud. Click Mount to place each one into the scene.

RGB-D Depth Sensor
640×480  |  0.3–8 m range
Captures a depth image where every pixel stores a distance value Z. This depth map is the raw input that gets back-projected into 3D.
Sensor Mount Arm
Fixed position  |  Adjustable height
Holds the sensor rigidly in place during the scan. Stability here ensures the intrinsic parameters from calibration remain valid.
Scan Target (Cube)
20×20×20 cm reference object
A simple geometric object placed in the scene. Used to observe how resolution and noise affect the reconstructed point cloud shape.
Background Wall
Flat plane  |  8 m fixed distance
A flat surface behind the object. The boundary between object and wall is where flying pixel artefacts typically appear.

Mount the Depth Sensor

The sensor is fixed on its mount, aimed at the scene containing the cube and background wall. Its calibrated intrinsic parameters (from Experiment 2) determine how depth pixels convert to 3D coordinates.

What you observe in the 3D scene

The sensor body appears on the mount arm. The translucent blue frustum shows its field of view — every pixel inside this cone will receive a depth measurement when the scan is captured.

Arrange the Scene

Position the cube between the sensor and the background wall. The distance you choose here determines the point spacing and density you will observe once the scan is captured.

Observe in 3D scene

The cube moves toward or away from the sensor as you adjust distance. Notice that the field-of-view cone covers a wider physical area at greater distances — the same angular spread now spans more real-world metres.
